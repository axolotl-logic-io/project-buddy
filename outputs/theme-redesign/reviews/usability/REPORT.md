# Theme redesign usability review

The most consequential changes are shared across all three appearances. Preserve the existing spell and quest editing controls, make the latest conversation and reply status visible beside the composer, and keep the existing dialog and drawer behavior when adopting the new styles. Settings also needs a compact swatch row on phones.

## Scope and method

Reviewed revision `db79f47e9dd0b290cbbc870f7ddfb973114f2617` in installed Playwright Chromium 156.0.8078.4 on macOS. I opened local HTML at 1280 × 900 and 360 × 900 CSS pixels in Ivory Vellum, Soot & Brass and Espresso Leather. I compared the first viewport for each available exact capture and inspected selected rendered controls, accessibility roles and source markup. I also probed Settings and the site panel at 320 px. Keyboard checks used Tab, arrow keys and Escape on selected controls. The narrow interaction runs enabled touch emulation and used scripted clicks and keyboard input. Physical touch, on-screen keyboards, screen readers and cross-browser behavior remain unassessed.

The fixtures represent an email-link visitor and a signed-in user starting or resuming quests, organizing work, customizing spells, using the Timer and changing appearance. I treated Mara as the quest owner and the author of the editable spell. I inferred that the app will retain current React behavior where the design only changes classes. I separated omitted fixture data and unsupported prototype interactions from demonstrated issues in the proposed layout or markup.

Of 174 rendered view combinations, 153 had exact current screenshots. The remaining 21 are listed in the matrix. The two specimens each contain all three appearances on one page. I opened both specimens at each width and inspected their initial viewport and relevant component markup. Individual specimen states below the fold did not receive an exhaustive visual or interaction pass. The comparison sheets put current and proposed side by side for each appearance, in order from Ivory Vellum to Espresso Leather.

## Findings

### 1. Spell editing loses existing controls and three reply lists

- **Task and location** — Edit an existing spell in `spell--editor`.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_customize_spell` in [Spells](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/spellbook.md).
- **Evidence** — Open the editor and choose “Reply when stopped.” It stays unselected and the panel still contains “Replies when cast,” at both widths in all appearances. Only the cast list exists in the mockup. Its rows have decorative grips and Remove buttons. The current captured HTML has named reorder and row-action buttons, an edit reply control and Copy and Forget actions. The proposed layout omits these controls. [1280 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-1280-24.jpg) · [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-24.jpg)
- **User impact** — A user cannot see how to edit an existing reply, reorder a reply by keyboard, or copy or forget the spell. The prototype cannot show how three other reply lists fit or how their validation is surfaced.
- **Severity** — Task blocker in the mockup. The corresponding app risk comes from replacing rather than styling the existing controls.
- **Recommendation** — Style the existing tab panels and editable-list controls. Include at least one alternative reply list with its edit and keyboard reorder controls, and show where Copy and Forget land at both widths.

### 2. The pinned composer offers to stop a reply whose status starts below the fold

- **Task and location** — Resume a quest, read the latest reply or decide whether to stop it in `quest--conversation` and `quest--loading-reply`.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_continue_chat`, `use_case_follow_latest_messages`, `use_case_stop_reply` in [Conversations](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/chats.md).
- **Evidence** — Load the waiting view at the top of the page. In all six combinations the pinned composer shows “Stop reply” while the pending `look` message and “Vellum is thinking…” sit later in the document. At 360 px, even the preceding reply’s progress link starts around y=2050 in a 900 px viewport. The current waiting captures show the pending exchange beside the composer. The new text label for Stop reply improves recognition once the state is in view. [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-8.jpg) · [1280 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-1280-8.jpg)
- **User impact** — Users see how to interrupt a reply before they see what is running. Resuming a quest presents older messages above a composer ready for new work.
- **Severity** — Substantial friction.
- **Recommendation** — Keep the app’s follow-latest behavior and its rule for users who scroll back. Give the waiting state a visible relationship to the composer and show a realistic latest-message viewport in the mockups. Keep a reachable “jump to latest” when reading history.

### 3. Quest collections omit the controls for correcting and revisiting work

- **Task and location** — Edit or reorder an existing task, reopen a finished task, or edit an idea in the Quest sidebar or phone drawer.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_tasks_panel` in [Tasks](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/tasks.md) and `use_case_quest_ideas` in [Ideas](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/ideas.md).
- **Evidence** — Open `quest--row-menu` or `quest--sidebar-drawer`. The proposed task titles are plain text. The opened row menu presents Move up, Move down and Remove task. The current Quest capture exposes named inline Edit and Reorder controls. Expand “Done (2)” in the mockup source state and the disclosure has no rows. Ideas retain Move to Tasks but omit the current captured Edit, Reorder and row-action controls. These omissions are shared markup across both widths and all modes. Spells & status and Members are also empty disclosures and cannot establish that their existing contents will fit. [360 px Tasks comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-12.jpg) · [1280 px Ideas comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-1280-8.jpg)
- **User impact** — People can identify and check work off, but the proposed screen gives them fewer ways to fix a typo, change the order, or revisit a task after checking it off.
- **Severity** — Substantial friction.
- **Recommendation** — Keep the existing editable row and completed-task controls when applying the shared classes. Include a populated Done disclosure and editable task and idea rows in the companion mockups.

### 4. Confirmation focus can leave the dialog, and the phone layout leads with Delete

- **Task and location** — Review or cancel milestone deletion in `quest--confirm-dialog`.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_edit_milestone` in [Quests](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/quests.md) and milestone deletion in [Progress timeline](../../../../docs/progress-timeline.md).
- **Evidence** — On load, focus is on the body. Press Tab once and focus goes to the underlying Project Buddy link in all six combinations. The displayed `alertdialog` has a name and description, but the prototype leaves background controls keyboard reachable. The current captured Radix markup hides the background from assistive technology. At 360 px, `wrap-reverse` puts Delete milestone above Keep it while DOM order starts with Keep it. The current phone capture shows Keep it above Delete milestone. At 1280 px the mockup shows both on one row. [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-4.jpg)
- **User impact** — Keyboard users can navigate an obscured page while a destructive decision is open. On phones the first visual action has the irreversible consequence, and keyboard order differs from visual order.
- **Severity** — Substantial friction.
- **Recommendation** — Keep Radix alert dialog behavior, focus Keep it on entry and restore focus to the invoking row on dismissal. Set explicit phone layout and action order. Apply the same focus check to the Quick Note dialog, whose prototype is also a static overlay.

### 5. Settings breaks the compact swatch row

- **Task and location** — Compare all appearances in Settings on a phone.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_choose_theme` and `use_case_use_on_phone` in [Appearance](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/appearance.md).
- **Evidence** — At 360 px and 320 px the Settings choices measure 96 × 96 px and wrap two-plus-one in all three appearances. The current 360 px settings captures keep all three in one row. The three 60 × 60 px site-panel choices fit one row at both phone widths. Tab/arrow-key probes showed named radio selection and a distinct dashed outline in Settings. Escape left the focused tooltip visible, consistent with the mockup script having no handler for the component CSS’s dismissal attribute. [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-16.jpg)
- **User impact** — The two surfaces give different layouts for the same action. The extra Settings row breaks the brief’s 320 px compact-row requirement and makes the last choice easier to overlook.
- **Severity** — Minor friction.
- **Recommendation** — Use the compact variant when the Settings container cannot fit three large choices. Preserve the full clickable label and the existing Escape dismissal and touch-tooltip logic.

### 6. Signed-out visitors lose the appearance entry point

- **Task and location** — Choose an appearance before entering an email or after requesting a sign-in link.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_visit_signed_out`, `use_case_sign_in_by_link` and `use_case_choose_theme` in [Access](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/access.md), [Accounts](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/accounts.md) and [Appearance](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/appearance.md).
- **Evidence** — Both sign-in mockups render only the header and card, with no Site panel control. Current screenshots include the breadcrumb-row panel trigger and the captured HTML includes the three named appearance radios. The prototype gallery switcher is a review control outside the page. The sent state also replaces the displayed email field, “Email me a link” and Sign up with “Use a different email address,” while retaining the 30-minute notice. [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-20.jpg) · [1280 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-1280-20.jpg)
- **User impact** — A first-time visitor cannot choose their preferred appearance through the page. From the sent state, they can no longer confirm the address they entered at a glance or request another link directly.
- **Severity** — Substantial friction for appearance access. The sent-state changes need a product decision.
- **Recommendation** — Keep the signed-out panel and its appearance controls. Decide whether the replacement sent state should keep the recipient visible and expose a resend path.

### 7. The phone drawer reports expanded while closed

- **Task and location** — Find and open the quest collections from the phone conversation.
- **Use case and reference** — `use_case_use_on_phone` and `use_case_tasks_panel` in [Appearance](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/appearance.md) and [Tasks](../../../../apps/web/use-cases/tasks.md).
- **Evidence** — At 360 px all three conversation mockups hide the drawer while the Quest button already exposes `aria-expanded="true"`. A click opens the drawer and the attribute stays true. Escape leaves the drawer open in the prototype. The capture names the closed control “Open quest sidebar.” The mockup’s short “Quest” label provides less guidance when read alone. At 1280 px the drawer treatment becomes an open sticky sidebar. [360 px comparison](/private/tmp/pb-usability-QOuaWT/compare-360-12.jpg)
- **User impact** — The control reports the wrong state before it is used. A keyboard user cannot dismiss it with Escape in the prototype.
- **Severity** — Substantial friction.
- **Recommendation** — Keep distinct desktop sidebar and phone drawer state in the existing controller. Derive visibility and expanded state from the same value, preserve a descriptive control name, and keep the existing keyboard close and focus return behavior.

## Implementability and review limits

The component catalogue maps old classes to proposed classes and React owners. The shared component stylesheet uses semantic paint roles and puts ornament in mask slots. I found no theme selector that changes component layout in that stylesheet. The swatch face intentionally sets its own appearance so it previews the correct texture, while the shared component owns the layout. The mapping is detailed enough to style the current React controls in place, including the Radix tabs, menus and dialogs. The simplified HTML is a weaker source for behavior and accessible names.

The catalogue promises Radix arrow navigation, modal containment and tooltip dismissal. These are implementation requirements even though the screenshot fixtures skip parts of them. The proposed styles also depend on shared chrome-height variables for sticky content and drawer placement. At intermediate widths, a long breadcrumb wrapping above the drawer could make those offsets inaccurate if they are fixed. This is a hypothesis. I did not test 768 px or substitute a narrower viewport for a zoom test. I also did not verify production contrast, layout shift, load performance, resilience, browser persistence, reduced-motion behavior in a live app or every hover and pressed state.

The brief approves swatch ornament over the older texture-only rule. There are two other choices to clarify before implementation. The mobile brand in the captures shows the medallion alone, while the mockups add the Project Buddy wordmark. The sign-in sent layout removes controls and adds an address-change link. Either could be intentional, but each changes current product behavior or layout and should be accounted for explicitly.

The strongest usability features are the explicit Continue card, empty states that offer a next step, the named Send and Stop reply controls, field-specific error text and a shared three-choice appearance picker. These patterns carry across all appearances. No application process, accounts, email, or database data were created. The report is the only workspace change from this review. Screenshot evidence remains in the temporary directory.
